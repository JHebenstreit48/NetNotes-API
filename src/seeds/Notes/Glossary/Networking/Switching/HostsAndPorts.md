# Hosts and Devices

<hr class="dividerSection" />

## Host

<hr class="dividerSection" />

A <span class="emphasis">host</span> is any device connected to a network that sends or receives data.

<hr class="dividerSubsection2" />

### Examples

<hr class="dividerSubsection2" />

<div class="centeredBullet">
  <ul class="diamondBullets fullWidthBullet">
    <li>PCs</li>
    <li>Macs</li>
    <li>Smartphones</li>
    <li>IoT devices</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<hr class="dividerSubsection1" />

## Patch Cable

<hr class="dividerSubsection1" />

A <span class="emphasis">patch cable</span> is a short Ethernet cable used to connect devices within a network.

<hr class="dividerSubsection2" />

### Common Use Cases

<hr class="dividerSubsection2" />

<div class="centeredBullet">
  <ul class="diamondBullets fullWidthBullet">
    <li>A switch to a router</li>
    <li>A host (e.g., PC) to a switch</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<hr class="dividerSubsection2" />

### Common Categories

<hr class="dividerSubsection2" />

<div class="centeredBullet">
  <ul class="diamondBullets fullWidthBullet">
    <li><span class="codeSnip">Cat5e</span></li>
    <li><span class="codeSnip">Cat6</span></li>
    <li><span class="codeSnip">Cat6e</span> (note: not an official TIA standard, a manufacturer marketing term for enhanced Cat6 with 500-600 MHz bandwidth, performance varies by manufacturer)</li>
    <li><span class="codeSnip">Cat6a</span> (TIA-recognized standard, guarantees 10GbE up to 100 meters)</li>
    <li><span class="codeSnip">Cat7</span> (note: not a TIA standard, requires proprietary connectors, limited adoption)</li>
    <li><span class="codeSnip">Cat8</span> (data center use, short runs up to 30 meters)</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<hr class="dividerSection" />

## Ports and Connections

<hr class="dividerSection" />

## Router Port Naming

<hr class="dividerSection" />

Router interfaces follow a standardized naming format.

### Common Format

<hr class="dividerSubsection2" />

<div class="centeredBullet">
  <ul class="diamondBullets fullWidthBullet">
    <li><span class="codeSnip">GigabitEthernet0/0</span></li>
  </ul>
</div>

### The Name Typically Reflects

<hr class="dividerSubsection2" />

<div class="centeredBullet">
  <ul class="diamondBullets fullWidthBullet">
    <li>Interface type</li>
    <li>Speed class</li>
    <li>Slot, module, and port position (vendor-dependent)</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<hr class="dividerSubsection1" />

## Ethernet Ports

<hr class="dividerSubsection1" />

A general term for ports that use Ethernet standards for Layer 2 connectivity.

<hr class="dividerSubsection1" />

## LAN Ports

<hr class="dividerSubsection1" />

Ports intended for connecting devices within a Local Area Network.

Often used as a label on consumer routers to distinguish internal ports from the WAN or uplink port.

<hr class="dividerSubsection1" />

## RJ-45 Ports

<hr class="dividerSubsection1" />

Refers to the physical connector type commonly used for twisted-pair Ethernet.

<hr class="dividerSubsection2" />

### Commonly Supports

<hr class="dividerSubsection2" />

<div class="centeredBullet">
  <ul class="diamondBullets fullWidthBullet">
    <li><span class="codeSnip">Cat5e</span></li>
    <li><span class="codeSnip">Cat6</span></li>
    <li><span class="codeSnip">Cat6a</span></li>
    <li><span class="codeSnip">Cat8</span> (also uses RJ-45, unlike Cat7)</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<hr class="dividerSubsection1" />

## SFP (Small Form-factor Pluggable)

<hr class="dividerSubsection1" />

A port type and pluggable transceiver system used for flexible media choices.

<hr class="dividerSubsection2" />

### Common Media Options

<hr class="dividerSubsection2" />

<div class="centeredBullet">
  <ul class="diamondBullets fullWidthBullet">
    <li>Fiber optic modules</li>
    <li>Copper modules (including RJ-45-based modules, depending on platform)</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<hr class="dividerSubsection2" />

### Types

<hr class="dividerSubsection2" />

<div class="centeredBullet">
  <ul class="diamondBullets fullWidthBullet">
    <li><span class="codeSnip">SFP</span>, commonly up to 1 Gbps</li>
    <li><span class="codeSnip">SFP+</span>, commonly up to 10 Gbps</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<hr class="dividerSubsection1" />

## LC Connector

<hr class="dividerSubsection1" />

<div class="centeredBullet">
  <ul class="diamondBullets fullWidthBullet">
    <li><span class="codeSnip">LC</span> (Lucent Connector):</li>
    <li>Small form-factor fiber connector commonly used in high-density environments.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<hr class="dividerSubsection1" />

## SC Connector

<hr class="dividerSubsection1" />

<div class="centeredBullet">
  <ul class="diamondBullets fullWidthBullet">
    <li><span class="codeSnip">SC</span> (Subscriber/Standard Connector):</li>
    <li>Larger push-pull fiber connector commonly seen in older installs and some residential or structured cabling.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<hr class="dividerSubsection1" />

## Switch Ports

<hr class="dividerSubsection1" />

A general term for physical interfaces on a switch.

<hr class="dividerSubsection1" />

## Access Ports

<hr class="dividerSubsection1" />

Switch ports configured to carry traffic for a single VLAN (common end-host port behavior).

Typically used for endpoints like PCs, printers, phones, and access points (depending on design).

<hr class="dividerSubsection1" />

## Console Ports

<hr class="dividerSubsection1" />

Used for direct device management and initial configuration.

<hr class="dividerSubsection2" />

### Common Connector Types

<hr class="dividerSubsection2" />

<div class="centeredBullet">
  <ul class="diamondBullets fullWidthBullet">
    <li><span class="codeSnip">RJ-45</span></li>
    <li><span class="codeSnip">USB</span> (USB-A or USB-C)</li>
  </ul>
</div>

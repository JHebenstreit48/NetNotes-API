# What Counts as a Connected Device on Your Network

<hr class="dividerSection" />

### What Are Connected Devices?

<hr class="dividerSection" />

A <span class="emphasis">connected device</span> is any device that communicates on a network using wired or wireless connectivity.

In a home or small office environment, most connected devices are considered <span class="emphasis">end hosts</span> because they generate or consume data rather than forwarding traffic for other devices.

These devices connect to the <span class="emphasis">LAN (Local Area Network)</span> side of a router or switch.

### Common Types of Connected Devices

<hr class="dividerSection" />

Home and small office networks commonly include:

<div class="centeredBullet">
  <ul class="diamondBullets fullWidthBullet">
    <li>Desktop computers</li>
    <li>Laptops</li>
    <li>Gaming consoles</li>
    <li>Smart TVs</li>
    <li>Printers and scanners</li>
    <li>Security cameras</li>
    <li>Smart speakers</li>
    <li>Telephones (VoIP or Wi-Fi calling)</li>
    <li>Climate control systems (smart thermostats)</li>
    <li>Mobile devices (smartphones and tablets)</li>
  </ul>
</div>

As new technologies emerge, more household functions rely on network connectivity for monitoring, automation, and remote control.

<hr class="dividerSection" />

### Wired vs Wireless Devices

<hr class="dividerSection" />

Connected devices may attach to the network in different ways:

<table class="notesTable">
  <thead>
    <tr class="tableHeader">
      <th class="tableCellHeader">Connection Type</th>
      <th class="tableCellHeader">Description</th>
    </tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr class="tableRow">
      <td class="tableCell">Wired (Ethernet)</td>
      <td class="tableCell">Connected using an RJ-45 Ethernet cable to a LAN port on a router or switch.</td>
    </tr>
    <tr class="tableRow">
      <td class="tableCell">Wireless (Wi-Fi)</td>
      <td class="tableCell">Connected through the router's built-in wireless access point.</td>
    </tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

In most home networks, wired and wireless devices share the same LAN and can communicate with each other directly.

  <span class="emphasis">See:</span>
  <a href="/foundations/basics/devices-and-models/routers/interfaces-and-port-types" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Foundations → Devices & Models → Routers → Interfaces and Port Types</a>
</div>

<hr class="dividerSection" />

### Infrastructure vs End Devices

<hr class="dividerSection" />

Not all devices on a network serve the same purpose.

<table class="notesTable">
  <thead>
    <tr class="tableHeader">
      <th class="tableCellHeader">Device Category</th>
      <th class="tableCellHeader">Purpose</th>
    </tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr class="tableRow">
      <td class="tableCell">End Devices</td>
      <td class="tableCell">Generate and consume data (PCs, TVs, phones, IoT devices).</td>
    </tr>
    <tr class="tableRow">
      <td class="tableCell">Infrastructure Devices</td>
      <td class="tableCell">Forward, manage, or secure traffic (routers, switches, access points).</td>
    </tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

Infrastructure devices connect networks together, while end devices primarily use network services.

  <span class="emphasis">See:</span>
  <a href="/foundations/basics/devices-and-models/end-devices/introduction" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Foundations → Devices & Models → End Devices → Introduction</a>
</div>

### Growth of IoT in Home Networks

<hr class="dividerSection" />

Modern networks increasingly support <span class="emphasis">Internet of Things (IoT)</span> devices.

Examples include:

<div class="centeredBullet">
  <ul class="diamondBullets fullWidthBullet">
    <li>Smart lighting systems</li>
    <li>Smart locks</li>
    <li>Video doorbells</li>
    <li>Environmental sensors</li>
    <li>Voice assistants</li>
  </ul>
</div>

The growth of IoT increases:

<div class="centeredBullet">
  <ul class="diamondBullets fullWidthBullet">
    <li>The number of connected devices</li>
    <li>Bandwidth demand</li>
    <li>Security considerations</li>
  </ul>
</div>

As networks expand, proper segmentation and secure configuration become more important.

<hr class="dividerSection" />

### Relationship to WLAN

<hr class="dividerSection" />

In home environments, many devices connect wirelessly using a <span class="emphasis">Wireless Local Area Network (WLAN)</span>.

Wireless devices:

<div class="centeredBullet">
  <ul class="diamondBullets fullWidthBullet">
    <li>Associate with a wireless access point</li>
    <li>Receive an IP address from the router</li>
    <li>Join the same local network as wired devices (by default)</li>
  </ul>
</div>
